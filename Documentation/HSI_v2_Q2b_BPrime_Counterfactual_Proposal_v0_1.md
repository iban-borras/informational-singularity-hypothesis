# Q2b B-Prime Counterfactual Proposal v0.1

Status: draft proposal, not executed.

Date: 2026-05-19

## Motivation

Hostile-review Q2 asks whether the golden-ratio fingerprints of canonical B are
an analytic artifact of its specific local collapse rule rather than a wider
property of the HSI Level 0 mechanism.

Q2a concludes that canonical B is not equivalent to a fixed Fibonacci-style
morfic substitution under the current executable semantics. Q2b therefore asks
for an empirical counterfactual:

> Does a structurally different collapse rule, still satisfying the necessary
> D1--D5 mechanism constraints, preserve the quintuple signature without the
> same phi convergence?

## Proposed B-prime

Name:

```text
Bxor
```

Short label:

```text
B'
```

The counterfactual keeps the canonical B outer mechanism:

1. binary alphabet over observable `0/1` plus structural parentheses;
2. monotonic accumulation of the historical trace;
3. decay frame `(Acc)1`;
4. deterministic inside-to-out collapse;
5. intermediate collapse states appended to the accumulated trace;
6. final global simplification when needed.

The only change is the local simplifier.

## Local simplifier

Canonical B local simplifier is AND-like after pair degradation:

```text
s_B(w) = 1 iff all observable bits in w are 1
s_B(w) = 0 otherwise
```

Proposed Bxor local simplifier:

```text
s_Bxor(w) = sum(w_i) mod 2
```

Equivalently:

- an even number of `1` symbols collapses to `0`;
- an odd number of `1` symbols collapses to `1`;
- empty observable content is not expected under the B frame; if encountered,
  it collapses to `0`.

This is not the canonical `01,10 -> 0` degradation rule. It is a parity-style
annihilation rule with no tunable threshold.

## Why Bxor rather than a threshold rule

Threshold rules such as majority, supermajority, or "at least two thirds" would
introduce a free numerical parameter. That would immediately reopen the same
hostile-review concern: the result might depend on the chosen threshold.

Bxor has no fitted scalar parameter. It is the simplest algebraic alternative
that:

- preserves binary tension;
- preserves determinism;
- preserves inside-to-out stratification;
- preserves accumulation;
- changes the local annihilation semantics strongly enough to test whether the
  canonical signature depends on the exact B rule.

## Outcome categories

### `signature-independent-of-phi-candidate`

Bxor preserves the compact-selective/quintuple signature at diagnostic scale but
does not converge to the same phi fingerprints.

Interpretation: the signature is not reducible to canonical B's phi side
fingerprint.

### `canonical-rule-dependent`

Bxor fails the compact-selective/quintuple signature.

Interpretation: the signature depends materially on canonical B's local collapse
semantics. This does not refute HSI, but it strengthens the hostile-review
concern that the current empirical signature is implementation-specific.

### `phi-persists-under-Bxor`

Bxor preserves both the signature and the phi fingerprints.

Interpretation: phi is not uniquely tied to canonical pair degradation. A deeper
analytic mechanism may be operating, but no stronger claim is authorized.

### `not-executable-under-v0.1`

Bxor does not produce enough observable material or violates the isolated
counterfactual contract.

Interpretation: no empirical conclusion; redesign requires a new proposal.
